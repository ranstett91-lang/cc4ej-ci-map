TECHNICAL MEMO: Census Block Group Boundary Failure
in Addicks Estates, Claymont, Delaware

RE: Structural data gap causing Addicks Estates to be undercounted in EPA EJScreen, DelDOT Equity Focus Area designations, and all federal cumulative impacts tools.

Prepared by: CC4EJ Cumulative Impacts Project
Issue: Census block group boundaries obscure the true environmental burden on Addicks Estates residents, systematically excluding this community from protective designations.

1. Background

Addicks Estates is a small residential neighborhood in Claymont, New Castle County, Delaware (ZIP 19703), entered via Myrtle Avenue east of Interstate 495. Residents live in direct proximity to:

  • The Citisteel / Phoenix Steel contamination site (DNREC VCP Site DE-0046) — 343 acres of former steel production land under active cleanup, with 7 designated Operable Units, directly adjacent to the neighborhood
  • Interstate 495 (Vietnam Veterans Memorial Highway) — a major freight corridor running along the western edge of the neighborhood
  • The Claymont Transit Center — a bus/rail hub generating additional diesel traffic at the neighborhood's edge
  • The INEOS Delaware Valley Works (formerly General Chemical) — a sulfuric acid manufacturing plant
  • The Honeywell / Allied Chemical AWE Superfund Site — federal Superfund cleanup site

2. The Data Gap

All major environmental justice screening tools — including EPA EJScreen, Delaware's own DNREC EJ Area Viewer, and the DelDOT Equity Focus Areas (EFA) 2024 — assess cumulative burden at the census block group (BG) level. Addicks Estates falls within Census Block Group 100030101051.

This block group does not represent Addicks Estates. Its geographic boundary extends:

  • North past Archmere Academy (a large private school campus with no resident population)
  • North past commercial corridors along Philadelphia Pike
  • Further north through the uninhabited industrial waterfront along the Delaware River
  • All the way to the Pennsylvania state border

The overwhelming majority of this block group's geographic area contains no residential population. The result: a small residential community bearing severe industrial burden is statistically averaged with empty land, school grounds, and commercial properties — systematically diluting every indicator used to identify and protect overburdened communities.

The true burden on residents — isolated by I-495 and surrounded by active contamination sites — is almost certainly higher than these diluted figures suggest.

4. Impact on Protective Designations

Block group boundary failure directly caused the following protective gaps:

  1. DelDOT Equity Focus Area (EFA) 2024 — The EFA designation for BG 100030101051 is classified as Significant, meaning DelDOT nominally identified it. However, the geographic extent of the block group means that transportation and infrastructure investments triggered by this designation may be directed to other parts of the BG rather than the specific Addicks Estates residential area.
  2. DNREC EJ Area Viewer / Permit Review — DNREC uses EJScreen data during permit application review. A new facility or permit modification that places Addicks Estates just outside the visible block group centroid may escape scrutiny, because the block group visually spans from the PA border to the Delaware River and its centroid does not correspond to the residential neighborhood.
  3. Federal Justice40 / CEJST — The Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool (CEJST) similarly uses census tract data. Addicks Estates' tract-level scores are similarly diluted by the large non-residential land area.

6. Requests and Recommendations

  1. DNREC Permit Review: Any DNREC permit review affecting the Claymont corridor should explicitly note that EJScreen data for BG 100030101051 underrepresents the Addicks Estates residential population due to block group boundary issues. Reviewers should weight the 99th percentile PM2.5 and 96th percentile traffic scores — not the geographic extent of the BG — when assessing cumulative impacts.
  2. U.S. Census Bureau: CC4EJ requests that the Census Bureau evaluate a block group boundary revision for the Addicks Estates area ahead of the 2030 decennial census. The current boundary conflates a residential neighborhood with uninhabited industrial waterfront land in a manner that structurally excludes the community from environmental protection frameworks.
  3. DelDOT EFA Program: We request that DelDOT review whether the geographic extent of BG 100030101051 is causing Addicks Estates to be functionally excluded from EFA-triggered investments, and that the EFA framework be updated to allow sub-block-group designations for communities demonstrably separated from the rest of their BG by non-residential land.
  4. DNREC EJ Policy: We urge DNREC to adopt supplemental guidance directing permit reviewers to consider community-documented data gaps when standard block group scores appear inconsistent with known local conditions — consistent with EPA's guidance on cumulative impacts and lived experience in environmental justice review.
