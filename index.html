Quick word list
Pollution Burden — pollution from factories, traffic, and waste sites that lands in this area. Higher = worse.
Vulnerability — how hard it is for people here to deal with health problems (money, jobs, language, age).

Upwind 💨 — the direction the wind is coming from. Pollution from upwind factories blows toward you.
AQI — Air Quality Index. 0–50 is clean; over 100 can hurt sensitive people; over 150 is unhealthy for everyone.

⚑ Claymont Focus (ZIP 19703)

About this area

Claymont is sandwiched between I-95, I-495, the Delaware River, and a dense industrial corridor — including a steel mill, sulfuric acid plant, specialty chemical plant, Honeywell Superfund site, and 343 acres of contaminated former steel land under active DNREC cleanup. Cross-state pollution from Marcus Hook, PA is also a named concern.

💧 Water Quality

Key water quality concerns

The Delaware River is the primary drinking water source for millions and receives industrial discharge from refineries, chemical plants, and wastewater facilities along the PA–NJ–DE corridor. PFAS contamination from Chemours (Edge Moor, DE and Chambers Works, NJ) has reached the Christina River and Delaware River. Naamans Creek — which runs along the 343-acre Citisteel contamination site in Claymont — remains impaired from heavy-metal runoff. Industrial NPDES water discharge permits for facilities like Delaware City Refinery are separate from air permits and are rarely flagged in community EJ reviews.

Veolia Delaware (NCC drinking water): EWG testing of the Veolia system serving ~100,000 NCC residents (system ID DE0000564) detected PFAS at up to 29 ppt average in source water (Red Clay & White Clay Creeks) — exceeding EPA's 4 ppt limit for PFOA/PFOS. A $42.5M GAC treatment plant opened March 2025; PFAS now non-detectable. Other contaminants found include disinfection byproducts (TTHMs, HAA5), chromium (hexavalent), and nitrate. Federal legal limits for many of these haven't been updated in nearly 20 years.

Combined Burden (0–10)

What the scores mean

Combined Burden (0–10)
A single score that mixes two things: how much pollution a neighborhood gets and how hard it is for people there to deal with it (money, jobs, language, age). A neighborhood only scores high if both are true — lots of pollution and fewer ways to cope.
0–3 = Low  ·  4–5 = Moderate  ·  6–7 = High  ·  8–10 = Extreme

Darker patches inside a neighborhood = Equity Focus Areas the state of Delaware has flagged as needing extra attention. A "Significant" flag counts more than a "Moderate" flag.

Uses the same combined-score idea as California's EnviroScreen tool.

⚠ Name ⚠ = Data gap neighborhood. Census block group boundaries include schools, commercial areas, or other non-residential land — or bundle multiple distinct neighborhoods — diluting the real burden on residents. Scores shown are likely lower than the true impact.
A dashed orange border on the map marks block groups with documented data gaps.

Observed health outcomes

About this health data
Observed rates come from CDC PLACES (BRFSS adult survey), reported at the census-tract level and reapplied to every block group within each tract. Neighborhood-scale variation inside a tract is not visible.
Adult-only (18+). Pediatric asthma is not represented at this resolution — the user observation that "kids on my block have asthma" cannot be confirmed or refuted by this dataset. CC4EJ is requesting sub-county pediatric release from DHSS.
The modeled air-toxics cancer risk and respiratory hazard percentiles shown in the air section above are exposure estimates, not observed disease — they are intentionally separated from observed prevalence here.
Block-group boundaries may merge distinct neighborhoods — see the data-gap notes for Addicks Estates, Aniline Village, Hickman Row, and Knollwood.

At-risk populations

About at-risk populations
County-level population counts and health-condition rates for groups most vulnerable to air pollution, following the American Lung Association State of the Air methodology. Children under 18, adults 65+, and people with asthma, COPD, or cardiovascular disease are more affected by ozone and particle pollution than the general population. Values are identical for every block group in a county by design — this is a county-scale indicator, not a neighborhood one. Pediatric asthma prevalence is from CDC BRFSS Child ACBS (state rate) where available; CDC NHIS national rate is used as a fallback and is flagged inline. Fields showing “pending” need transcription from the SOTA county tables — run python3 scripts/build_at_risk_populations.py from the repo root to refresh.

    📚 Data & Methods

    Where the numbers come from. EPA, CDC, US Census, NOAA, DelDOT. Tap a section below to see the formula and the original source.

    Quick version: Pollution scores come from EPA EJScreen. Health outcomes come from CDC PLACES (BRFSS adult survey). Demographics come from the US Census. Climate forecasts come from NOAA. Equity-area flags come from DelDOT. Every number on this map traces back to a public dataset. Full source list →
    Data sources
    • EPA EJScreen (2016–2024 releases) — block-group environmental & demographic percentiles. Drives PM2.5, diesel, cancer risk, traffic, Superfund proximity, and the modern demographic indicators. Not collected before 2016; the slider shows "air data not collected before 2016" in that range.
    • US Census Decennial + ACS 5-year (1970–2015 historical demographics) — pre-EJScreen block-group demographics (low-income, unemployment, less-than-HS, linguistic isolation, people of color) sourced from Decennial SF3/STF3 (1970/1980/1990/2000), Decennial 2010 + ACS 2006–2010, and ACS 5-year 2007–2011 through 2011–2015. Linguistic isolation as a household concept was introduced in 1990; earlier records show "—" for that row.
    • CDC PLACES (BRFSS 2020–2025 releases) — observed adult health outcomes (asthma, COPD, coronary heart disease, depression, stroke, …) at census-tract level, reapplied to every block group inside the tract. Not collected before 2020.
    • DelDOT Equity Focus Areas 2024 (Pennoni methodology) — transportation-equity designations, residential-land-use clipped.
    • EPA TRI — multi-year facility emissions (1987–2024).
    • DNREC Air Permits & VCP Sites — Delaware state permits and Voluntary Cleanup Program sites.
    • EPA RMP accident history · PHMSA · NRC — chemical-disaster incidents, 2004–2025.
    • NOAA GSOY — observed annual temperature & precipitation, 1970–2025 (county-level).
    • USGS NLCD 2021 Imperviousness — 30 m percent-developed-imperviousness raster, aggregated to block-group means. Drives the Heat overlay as a satellite-measured urban-heat-island proxy with no demographic input. Loaded from bg_heat_index.json when present; otherwise the overlay falls back to a demographic placeholder and the legend flags it as modeled.
    • CC4EJ Community Listening Sessions — locally-verified facility metadata.

    How Pollution Burden (EB) is scored

    Pollution Burden is a weighted mean of 10 EPA EJScreen state percentiles, rescaled to a 0–10 range. Higher weight = more influence on the score. Exact weights from ejscreen_pipeline.py:

    IndicatorWeight
    PM2.51.2
    Ozone1.0
    Diesel PM1.2
    Air-toxics cancer risk1.2
    Respiratory hazard1.0
    Traffic proximity1.0
    NPL Superfund proximity0.9
    RMP proximity0.9
    TSDF proximity0.8
    Wastewater discharge0.8

    EB = min(10, max(0, Σ(value × weight) / Σ(weights) / 10))

    How Social Vulnerability (SV) is scored

    SV is a two-part blend: 60% EJScreen socioeconomic percentiles + 40% observed CDC PLACES health relative to the Delaware state average.

    Indicator (EJScreen SV, 60% of SV)Weight
    Low income %1.3
    Unemployment1.0
    Linguistic isolation0.9
    Less than HS education0.9
    Age under 5 or over 640.8
    Life expectancy percentile0.9
    People of color %0.7

    Health component (40% of SV) scales asthma, coronary heart disease, depression, COPD, smoking, stroke, and mental-health tract prevalence against DE state averages, capped at 2.5× state.

    SV = 0.6 × sv_ej + 0.4 × sv_health

    Combined Burden and the "overburdened" threshold

    Combined Burden (CB) = √(EB × SV) — high only when both pollution and vulnerability are high, following the CalEnviroScreen pattern.

    Overburdened: block groups where EB ≥ 6.0 AND SV ≥ 6.0. In Delaware, that's 70 of 706 block groups (≈10%).

    Post-2025 projections — assumptions and limits

    Climate projections cover 2025–2100; the "What's coming" chip samples the mid-century (2050) benchmark. When the year is after 2025:

    • Block-group demographics are held at the 2024 EJScreen vintage. Year-over-year variation after 2024 is not modeled — it's the same snapshot repainted under different climate scenarios.
    • Sea-level rise uses DE Sea-Level Rise Technical Committee (2017) bins: RCP 4.5 reaches ~1 ft by 2050 / ~3 ft by 2100; RCP 8.5 reaches ~3 ft by 2050 / ~7 ft by 2100. This is a simplified lookup table, not a dynamic coastal model.
    • Heat overlay uses USGS NLCD 2021 percent-imperviousness per block group as a satellite-measured proxy for summer Land Surface Temperature (urban heat island). Independent of the demographic signals that define equity focus areas, so the overlay actually tests the EFA-runs-hotter hypothesis instead of re-encoding it. Year scaling still applies a global warming multiplier on top of the spatial pattern. If bg_heat_index.json hasn't been generated yet, the overlay falls back to a demographic placeholder and the legend badge flips from measured to modeled.
    • Extreme precipitation remains a placeholder. Sub-county rainfall in DE varies <10% statewide vs interannual swings >30%, so the planned replacement is a flood-exposure overlay (FEMA NFHL × NLCD imperviousness) rather than a downscaled rainfall raster. Treat the current overlay as directional only.
    • Infrastructure vulnerability ratings per asset × year × scenario are CC4EJ-curated estimates, not federal projections.

    In short: post-2025 colors on the fill layer reflect today's demographics; only climate overlays actually change. The projection chip is a scenario viewer, not a forecast.

    Known limitations
    • Tract-level health privacy floor. CDC PLACES is a census-tract dataset reapplied to every block group inside the tract — you cannot see neighborhood-scale variation within a tract, and pediatric asthma is not represented (BRFSS is adults 18+).
    • EJScreen percentiles are modeled exposures, not observed disease. Air-toxics cancer risk is an estimate, not a measured case rate.
    • Data-gap block groups. Some block groups bundle multiple distinct neighborhoods (Addicks Estates, Aniline Village, Hickman Row, Knollwood) — raw scores under-represent the burden on residents. These are flagged with ⚠ and a dashed outline.
    • DHSS ZIP-level health data (opioid overdose rates, state-sourced benchmarks) is not yet available at block-group resolution.
    • Ecological inference caveat. Exposure–health associations are peer-reviewed at the population level; this map cannot confirm that any individual's condition was caused by these exposures.
