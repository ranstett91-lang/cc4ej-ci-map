⚑ Claymont Focus (ZIP 19703)

About this area

Claymont is sandwiched between I-95, I-495, the Delaware River, and a dense industrial corridor — including a steel mill, sulfuric acid plant, specialty chemical plant, Honeywell Superfund site, and 343 acres of contaminated former steel land under active DNREC cleanup. Cross-state pollution from Marcus Hook, PA is also a named concern.

Combined Burden (0–10)

What the scores mean

Pollution Burden (0–10)
How much pollution a neighborhood is exposed to from all sources combined — air, water, toxic waste, traffic, and industrial facilities.
0–3 = Low  ·  4–5 = Moderate  ·  6–7 = High  ·  8–10 = Extreme

A score of 10 means the neighborhood faces more pollution than almost anywhere in Delaware — from factories, highways, contaminated land, and toxic air combined. A score of 0 means very little exposure to any of these sources.

Facility Severity (0–3)
How serious a single industrial facility's pollution is, based on the type of chemicals it releases and its size.
1–2 = Moderate  ·  2–2.5 = High  ·  2.5–3 = Extreme

Combined Burden (0–10)
Combines pollution exposure and community vulnerability into a single score: √(Pollution × Vulnerability). A neighborhood scores high only if it faces both heavy industrial pollution and limited resources to cope — the same logic as CalEnviroScreen's cumulative impact model.
0–3 = Low  ·  4–5 = Moderate  ·  6–7 = High  ·  8–10 = Extreme

Darker patches within a block group mark DelDOT Equity Focus Areas — communities where DelDOT's own criteria confirm that residents face above-average cumulative burden. Significant EFA designation adds a larger boost than Moderate.

Vulnerability (0–10)
How hard it would be for residents to cope with health problems caused by pollution — based on income, unemployment, language barriers, and age. Higher = fewer resources to deal with the health impacts.

DelDOT Equity Areas
Communities Delaware's transportation agency flagged as needing extra investment. DelDOT's methodology (Pennoni, 2024) clips every block group to residential land use before calculating EFA thresholds — removing the dilution caused by industrial, commercial, and vacant land within block group boundaries. 44 block groups produced two separate residential patches; those sub-area outlines appear as darker polygons in this layer. Click any sub-area to see its designation and which neighborhoods it covers.

⚠ Name ⚠ = Data gap neighborhood. Census block group boundaries include schools, commercial areas, or other non-residential land — or bundle multiple distinct neighborhoods — diluting the real burden on residents. Scores shown are likely lower than the true impact.
On the DelDOT Equity Areas view, a dashed orange border marks block groups with documented data gaps.
Data sources: EPA EJScreen 2023, EPA TRI, CDC PLACES 2022, DelDOT EFA 2024, DNREC Air Permits & VCP Sites, CC4EJ Community Listening Sessions.
